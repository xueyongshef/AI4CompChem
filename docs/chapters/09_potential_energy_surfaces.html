Sticking point 01

I cannot tell a scan from a full surface.

Starting question “Is a bond-length plot the entire PES?”

A more useful question

Explain the difference between a potential energy surface and a one-dimensional bond-length scan for a polyatomic molecule. Then distinguish a fixed-coordinate scan from a relaxed scan, stating which variables can change.

Why this helps: It specifies the dimensionality and the constraints on the calculation.

Compare with an example answer

A PES assigns an energy to molecular geometries under a defined electronic description. A one-dimensional scan samples a selected coordinate. In a fixed-coordinate scan the remaining coordinates are held fixed; in a relaxed scan selected remaining coordinates are optimised under the scan constraint. The resulting curves need not coincide.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

Could a low point on a constrained scan still fail to be a full molecular minimum?

Check your understanding

Does a one-dimensional scan explore every internal degree of freedom?

Reveal the check answer

No. It follows a chosen slice or constrained path.

Sticking point 02

I confuse the sign of the force with the slope.

Starting question “Does a positive energy mean a positive force?”

A more useful question

For the toy potential U(x) = k*x^2/2 with k positive, explain F = -dU/dx. Determine the force direction at x greater than zero and distinguish the energy value from its gradient.

Why this helps: It gives an explicit potential and asks about direction instead of vague signs.

Compare with an example answer

Differentiating gives dU/dx = k*x, so F = -k*x. At positive x the force points toward decreasing x, back toward the minimum. Shifting all energies by a constant changes U but not its gradient or the force. The force is set by slope, not by the sign of the energy.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

What is the force at x = 0?

Check your understanding

If I add a constant to every energy, does the force change?

Reveal the check answer

No. The derivative of a constant is zero.

Sticking point 03

I use potential energy and free energy interchangeably.

Starting question “Is the lowest-energy geometry always the most populated?”

A more useful question

Explain the difference between a PES and a finite-temperature free-energy profile. Use the number of accessible configurations to explain why a potential-energy minimum alone does not determine a population.

Why this helps: It asks about what is averaged over and which thermodynamic information is missing.

Compare with an example answer

A PES describes energy at specified geometries. A free-energy profile incorporates statistical weighting over configurations compatible with a selected coordinate and conditions. A region can have favourable population because it contains many accessible configurations. Defining the ensemble, temperature, and coordinate is essential when interpreting that profile.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

What information would I need to turn a trajectory histogram into a free-energy estimate?

Check your understanding

Can a single geometry's potential energy alone establish its finite-temperature population?

Reveal the check answer

No. Accessible configurations and statistical conditions also matter.
