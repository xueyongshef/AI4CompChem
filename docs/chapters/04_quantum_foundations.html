Sticking point 01

I cannot tell a wavefunction from an electron density.

Starting question “Are orbitals and electron density the same?”

A more useful question

Explain the difference between a many-electron wavefunction, a molecular orbital, and electron density at an introductory level. Use their variables and normalisation to distinguish them, then ask me one check question.

Why this helps: It asks for explicit distinctions instead of an analogy that could hide them.

Compare with an example answer

A many-electron wavefunction depends on all electronic coordinates and spin variables. An orbital is a one-electron function used to build approximate descriptions. Electron density describes the expected number of electrons per volume at a position; integrating it over space gives the electron count. A single orbital's squared magnitude is not generally the total density.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

How is the density assembled from occupied orbitals in a closed-shell independent-particle picture?

Check your understanding

For a ten-electron system, what does the spatial integral of the electron density give?

Reveal the check answer

Ten electrons.

Sticking point 02

I do not understand why the nuclei are held fixed.

Starting question “Why can we ignore nuclear motion?”

A more useful question

Explain the Born–Oppenheimer approximation using a bond-length scan. Describe what is solved at each fixed geometry, how the nuclei can move later, and when separating electronic and nuclear motion becomes less reliable.

Why this helps: It asks about the approximation's role and limits, rather than treating nuclei as permanently frozen.

Compare with an example answer

At each chosen nuclear geometry, solve an electronic problem parametrised by those nuclear positions. Electronic energy together with nuclear repulsion defines an energy surface for nuclear motion. Nuclear motion is then treated on that surface in the usual single-surface picture. Electronic-state coupling can matter near degeneracies or other nonadiabatic situations.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

What quantity changes between the points of a bond-length scan?

Check your understanding

Does the approximation mean real nuclei never move?

Reveal the check answer

No. It separates parts of the description of their motion.

Sticking point 03

Negative energies seem to mean that something is wrong.

Starting question “Why is the total energy negative?”

A more useful question

Explain why an isolated molecule can have a negative total energy relative to the chosen zero. Then explain why comparing raw total energies of different molecular compositions does not establish which is more stable.

Why this helps: It asks about both the reference and the validity of a comparison.

Compare with an example answer

An energy's sign depends on its zero. For a typical isolated electronic-structure calculation, attractive binding can give a negative total energy relative to separated free charges. A larger molecule often has a more negative total energy simply because it contains more particles. Chemical stability requires a defined comparison, such as a balanced reaction with consistently computed energies.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

How would you define an energy change for a balanced dissociation reaction?

Check your understanding

Does the most negative total energy among molecules with different compositions identify the most stable substance?

Reveal the check answer

No. The comparison needs a chemically defined reference.
