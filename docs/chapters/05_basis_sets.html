Sticking point 01

I confuse the method with the basis set.

Starting question “Is a basis set another method?”

A more useful question

Using HF/6-31G, HF/cc-pVDZ, and B3LYP/6-31G, identify which part describes the electronic-structure approximation and which part describes the orbital expansion. Explain what is held fixed in each comparison.

Why this helps: It uses named examples to separate two independent choices.

Compare with an example answer

HF and B3LYP specify electronic-structure approximations. The basis supplies functions used to represent orbitals. Moving from HF/6-31G to HF/cc-pVDZ changes the basis; moving from HF/6-31G to B3LYP/6-31G changes the method while keeping that basis. A larger basis does not turn HF into a correlated wavefunction method.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

Which settings would you hold fixed in a basis-convergence study?

Check your understanding

What changes between HF/6-31G and HF/cc-pVDZ?

Reveal the check answer

The basis set.

Sticking point 02

Polarisation and diffuse functions sound similar.

Starting question “Which extra functions do I need?”

A more useful question

Explain polarisation and diffuse functions separately, using a bent neutral molecule and an anion as motivating examples. Describe what each adds to the orbital description and how I could check whether it matters for my target property.

Why this helps: It requests mechanisms and a property-specific check, rather than a universal recipe.

Compare with an example answer

Polarisation functions add angular flexibility so an orbital description can respond to molecular bonding. Diffuse functions extend spatial flexibility farther from nuclei, which can matter for anions and other spatially extended distributions. Their practical importance depends on the system and property. Compare suitable basis choices systematically rather than treating one addition as a substitute for the other.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

Why might a small change in total energy still accompany an important change in a property?

Check your understanding

Are diffuse functions simply another name for higher angular momentum functions?

Reveal the check answer

No. Radial extent and angular flexibility are different features.

Sticking point 03

A larger basis gave a different energy.

Starting question “Does a lower energy always mean a better answer?”

A more useful question

Explain when the variational principle predicts a non-increasing HF energy as a basis is enlarged. State the assumptions about nested spaces and comparable converged solutions, and explain why this does not prove chemical accuracy.

Why this helps: It asks for the conditions behind a familiar rule.

Compare with an example answer

For the same Hamiltonian and HF variational class, enlarging a nested orbital space cannot raise the globally minimised HF energy. Practical comparisons may use non-nested bases or different stationary SCF solutions. Even a converged basis expansion retains the method's approximations. Accuracy in a reaction energy or another property therefore needs its own assessment.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

How would you distinguish basis incompleteness from missing electron correlation?

Check your understanding

Does basis convergence remove HF's remaining electron-correlation error?

Reveal the check answer

No. Method and basis errors are distinct.
