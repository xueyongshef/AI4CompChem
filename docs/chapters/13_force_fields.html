Sticking point 01

I do not understand what a force field adds together.

Starting question “Is a force field just a Lennard–Jones potential?”

A more useful question

Describe a typical molecular force field using bond, angle, torsion, Lennard–Jones, and Coulomb terms. Explain why the topology and non-bonded exclusions or scaling rules must match the parameter set.

Why this helps: It asks for the whole model and the conventions connecting its parts.

Compare with an example answer

A molecular force field commonly combines bonded and non-bonded terms, with parameters and conventions designed to work together. Topology determines which interactions apply. Some nearby bonded pairs are excluded or scaled in non-bonded calculations. A Lennard–Jones term alone does not represent this entire molecular model.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

What would happen if I counted an interaction twice by ignoring the exclusions?

Check your understanding

Does a Lennard–Jones pair term include every bonded and electrostatic interaction?

Reveal the check answer

No. Those require their own specified terms and conventions.

Sticking point 02

I confuse sigma with the equilibrium separation.

Starting question “Why is the Lennard–Jones minimum not at sigma?”

A more useful question

For U(r) = 4*epsilon*((sigma/r)^12 - (sigma/r)^6), show briefly where the energy crosses zero and where its minimum occurs. Distinguish sigma from the minimum position and epsilon from a distance.

Why this helps: It specifies the convention, making the parameter meanings checkable.

Compare with an example answer

At r = sigma the two powers cancel, so U = 0. Setting the derivative to zero gives r_min = 2^(1/6)*sigma, approximately 1.122*sigma. At that separation U = -epsilon. Sigma is a length parameter, while epsilon sets the well depth in energy units.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

Which parameter would I change to deepen the well without moving its minimum?

Check your understanding

Is the energy at r = sigma equal to -epsilon?

Reveal the check answer

No. It is zero for the stated convention.

Sticking point 03

My force curve has the wrong sign or units.

Starting question “Can I use the potential energy as the force?”

A more useful question

For a pair potential U(r), explain the radial force convention F_r = -dU/dr. State the force units when energy is in kJ/mol and distance in nm, and relate the sign to attraction and repulsion.

Why this helps: It fixes the coordinate and units before discussing the derivative.

Compare with an example answer

The radial force is the negative derivative of energy with respect to separation. Its units are kJ/mol/nm for the stated quantities. With increasing r as the positive radial direction, a negative radial force is attractive and a positive one repulsive. At a potential minimum the derivative and radial force vanish.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

How could I check an analytic force against a small finite difference in energy?

Check your understanding

What is the radial force at a smooth potential minimum?

Reveal the check answer

Zero.
