Sticking point 01

I confuse charge with spin multiplicity.

Starting question “What spin should I use?”

A more useful question

For neutral H2O and neutral OH, explain electron count, charge, total spin, and multiplicity separately. Use their usual ground-state assignments and ask me to count the electrons before giving the answer.

Why this helps: It names the systems and distinguishes quantities that are easily confused.

Compare with an example answer

Neutral water has ten electrons and its usual ground state is a closed-shell singlet, with multiplicity 1. Neutral OH has nine electrons and its usual ground state is a doublet, with multiplicity 2. Charge measures net electric charge; multiplicity is 2S + 1. A neutral molecule can have unpaired electrons.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

What changes if I remove an electron from a neutral molecule?

Check your understanding

Does neutral always mean singlet?

Reveal the check answer

No. Neutral OH is a counterexample.

Sticking point 02

My molecule looks much too large or too small.

Starting question “Are my coordinates wrong?”

A more useful question

My XYZ structure has unexpected bond lengths after import. Help me check coordinate units, atom order, and file interpretation before changing the geometry. Explain what I can and cannot infer from a bare XYZ file.

Why this helps: It asks for checks of representation before changing the physical structure.

Compare with an example answer

Standard XYZ-style coordinates commonly use angstrom, but a bare coordinate list may not declare units or electronic state. Check the generating program, importer settings, and one known interatomic distance. A consistent scale error can suggest a unit mismatch; it is not proof that the molecule should be reoptimised.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

What additional information should accompany the XYZ file for a quantum calculation?

Check your understanding

Does a list of element symbols and coordinates uniquely specify charge and multiplicity?

Reveal the check answer

No. Those must be supplied separately.

Sticking point 03

I am unsure whether two geometries are different conformers.

Starting question “These two structures look different. Which is better?”

A more useful question

Two coordinate files describe an isolated water molecule. Explain how to distinguish a rigid rotation or translation from a change in internal geometry. Ask me to compare bond lengths and the bond angle before comparing energies.

Why this helps: It checks physical equivalence before treating a visual difference as chemistry.

Compare with an example answer

For an isolated molecule without an external field, a rigid rotation or translation preserves its internal geometry and physical energy. Compare interatomic distances and angles, or align the structures. A changed bond length or angle represents an internal change. Tiny computed differences can also reflect numerical settings.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

What other information would you need before comparing two molecular conformers?

Check your understanding

Does translating all atoms by the same vector change their bond lengths?

Reveal the check answer

No. Pairwise separations remain the same.
