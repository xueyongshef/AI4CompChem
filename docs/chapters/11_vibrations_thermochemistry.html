Sticking point 01

I do not understand how many vibrations a molecule has.

Starting question “Why is the mode count different for water and CO2?”

A more useful question

Count vibrational modes for isolated nonlinear H2O and linear CO2. Explain the translational and rotational degrees of freedom and distinguish the number of modes from the number of distinct frequencies.

Why this helps: It supplies molecular geometry and separates two ways of counting.

Compare with an example answer

A nonlinear N-atom molecule has 3N - 6 vibrational modes, giving three for water. A linear molecule has 3N - 5, giving four for CO2. Two CO2 bending modes are degenerate in the ideal linear molecule, so four modes need not mean four distinct frequencies.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

How would you classify the two degenerate bending motions?

Check your understanding

How many vibrational modes does linear CO2 have?

Reveal the check answer

Four, including two degenerate bending modes.

Sticking point 02

A small imaginary frequency worries me.

Starting question “Should I delete the imaginary frequency?”

A more useful question

My intended minimum has a small imaginary frequency. Explain how the mode, geometry convergence, numerical settings, and possible constraints affect interpretation. Ask me to inspect the mode before deciding what action to take.

Why this helps: It requests a diagnosis rather than discarding inconvenient evidence.

Compare with an example answer

An imaginary harmonic frequency corresponds to negative curvature in the analysed vibrational direction. A small value can require checks of numerical accuracy or geometry convergence; it must not simply be deleted. Inspect the motion, any constraints, and relevant settings, then determine whether a genuine unstable direction remains.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

What would a large imaginary mode involving a substantial structural change suggest?

Check your understanding

Does a small imaginary frequency alone prove that the intended minimum is acceptable?

Reveal the check answer

No. Its origin and mode need investigation.

Sticking point 03

I confuse electronic energy with Gibbs free energy.

Starting question “Can I call my electronic-energy difference a free energy?”

A more useful question

Explain the contributions required to compare molecular Gibbs free energies from electronic-structure calculations. State temperature, pressure or standard state, and the treatment of low-frequency modes, and separate these from the electronic energy.

Why this helps: It asks for the definition and assumptions of the quantity being reported.

Compare with an example answer

A molecular thermochemistry model combines electronic energy with zero-point and thermal contributions and entropy. Temperature, standard state, and approximations for translation, rotation, and vibration matter. Low-frequency modes can make the harmonic treatment sensitive. An electronic-energy difference alone is not a Gibbs free-energy difference.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

Which corrections cancel, and which must be computed, for my specific reaction?

Check your understanding

Can I report a bare electronic-energy barrier as a Gibbs free-energy barrier?

Reveal the check answer

No. It must be labelled as an electronic-energy barrier.
