Sticking point 01

I heard DFT does not use orbitals.

Starting question “Why does my DFT output contain orbitals?”

A more useful question

Explain the distinction between electron density and the auxiliary orbitals of Kohn–Sham DFT. Compare this with HF at an introductory level without treating the two energy expressions as identical.

Why this helps: It names the formulation and resolves an apparent contradiction in the output.

Compare with an example answer

Kohn–Sham DFT commonly uses auxiliary orbitals to construct the density and part of the energy. Its exchange-correlation contribution is described through a functional, approximated in practical calculations. Thus, 'DFT uses density' does not mean a Kohn–Sham program never solves orbital equations.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

Which parts of a practical Kohn–Sham calculation depend on the chosen functional?

Check your understanding

Is seeing orbitals in a DFT output inconsistent with Kohn–Sham DFT?

Reveal the check answer

No. Orbitals are part of that formulation.

Sticking point 02

I do not know which settings are being compared.

Starting question “Why do two DFT calculations disagree?”

A more useful question

Help me compare two DFT calculations on the same molecule. Separate geometry and electronic state, functional, basis, grid, dispersion treatment, and convergence settings before interpreting the energy difference.

Why this helps: It prevents several changed settings being mistaken for a single effect.

Compare with an example answer

A difference can reflect the physical setup, model, or numerical approximation. Make a table of these settings and identify which vary. To isolate a functional change, keep the other relevant choices controlled and document any functional-dependent conventions. No particular cause can be diagnosed without the actual inputs and outputs.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

Which settings would you vary first to check numerical convergence?

Check your understanding

Is the integration grid the same thing as the orbital basis?

Reveal the check answer

No. They serve different roles.

Sticking point 03

An AI recommends one functional for everything.

Starting question “Which functional is the best?”

A more useful question

I want to predict [property] for [system]. Ask me about bonding, charge, spin, environment, and available benchmarks before discussing functional choices. Explain the evidence and limits behind any recommendation.

Why this helps: It supplies a target and requests evidence instead of a universal ranking.

Compare with an example answer

A useful recommendation depends on the chemical problem and property. Performance on one benchmark does not establish performance on every bonding regime or observable. First define the task, then examine relevant validation and practical settings. A functional's name or popularity is not sufficient evidence.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

What benchmark would resemble my intended application closely enough to be useful?

Check your understanding

Can one benchmark score guarantee the best functional for every molecule and property?

Reveal the check answer

No. The evidence must be relevant to the intended task.
