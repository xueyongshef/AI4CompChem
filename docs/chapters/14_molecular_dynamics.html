Sticking point 01

My NVE total energy drifts.

Starting question “Should I just add a thermostat?”

A more useful question

My intended NVE simulation shows total-energy drift. Help me design checks of time step, integration settings, constraints, force evaluation, and initial equilibration. Explain why adding a thermostat changes the diagnostic problem.

Why this helps: It preserves the intended ensemble and requests a controlled investigation.

Compare with an example answer

In an ideal isolated conservative system, total energy is conserved. Numerical integration and force approximations can cause deviations. Compare appropriately equilibrated runs at smaller time steps and inspect relevant settings and energy traces. A thermostat exchanges energy with a bath and changes the ensemble; it can hide a symptom without identifying its cause.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

What would a systematic reduction of drift with time step suggest, and what would it not prove?

Check your understanding

Does stable temperature after adding a thermostat prove that the original NVE integration was accurate?

Reveal the check answer

No. The thermostat can exchange energy.

Sticking point 02

The thermostat does not keep every temperature value identical.

Starting question “Why is the temperature fluctuating in NVT?”

A more useful question

Explain instantaneous kinetic temperature and canonical fluctuations. Distinguish a target temperature from a value that must be identical at every frame, and explain what diagnostics depend on the thermostat used.

Why this helps: It distinguishes a statistical target from an instantaneous constraint.

Compare with an example answer

An NVT simulation aims to sample a distribution at a specified temperature. Instantaneous kinetic energy, and therefore a kinetic-temperature estimate, generally fluctuates. The expected behaviour depends on system size, degrees of freedom, and thermostat algorithm. Judge averages, fluctuations, and sampling rather than requiring every frame to equal the target.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

How would changing the number of degrees of freedom affect my interpretation?

Check your understanding

Does NVT require the instantaneous kinetic temperature to be exactly constant?

Reveal the check answer

No. Canonical sampling includes fluctuations.

Sticking point 03

I do not know whether a trajectory is equilibrated.

Starting question “My trajectory is long. Is it enough?”

A more useful question

Help me assess equilibration and sampling for [observable] in [system]. Ask for time traces, initial conditions, run length, and independent runs. Explain why one apparently stable trace cannot prove global equilibrium.

Why this helps: It names the observable and asks for evidence of both relaxation and exploration.

Compare with an example answer

Inspect relevant observables for initial relaxation and compare production estimates across blocks and independent starts. A system may remain trapped in one region while a trace appears stationary. Adequate sampling is property-dependent; elapsed simulation time alone does not establish it.

Course-authored teaching example. Your AI response may differ.

Follow-up to ask

Which slow process could affect my observable without being obvious in the total-energy trace?

Check your understanding

Can a flat-looking energy trace prove that all important conformations were sampled?

Reveal the check answer

No. Other slow degrees of freedom may remain unexplored.
